One of the most frequently asked questions from South African SMMEs entering the government tender space is: **can I use a sworn affidavit instead of a formal B-BBEE certificate?**

The short answer: sometimes yes, sometimes no — and getting this wrong will disqualify your bid. This guide explains the rules clearly.

## The Legal Framework

B-BBEE measurement in the procurement context is governed by two overlapping pieces of legislation:

1. **The Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment Act, 53 of 2003** (as amended by Act 46 of 2013)
2. **The B-BBEE Codes of Good Practice** (gazetted under that Act, most recently revised in 2023)
3. **The Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act, Act 5 of 2000** and its 2022 Regulations

Together, these create the rules for when affidavits are acceptable and when formal verification is required.

## What Is an EME?

**EME stands for Exempted Micro Enterprise** — a business with an **annual total revenue of R10 million or less**.

EMEs are "exempted" because their compliance with the full B-BBEE scorecard would be administratively disproportionate. The law recognises that small businesses cannot afford R-rated verification agencies and should not be required to go through the full scorecard exercise.

### Default B-BBEE Levels for EMEs

| EME Category | Ownership | Default B-BBEE Level |
|---|---|---|
| Owned by person(s) with disability | Any | Level 1 |
| 51%+ black-owned | Yes | Level 1 |
| 30%+ black women-owned | Yes (≥30% women) | Level 2 |
| Other | No | Level 4 |

These are automatic — the EME does not need to be measured against the scorecard to claim these levels.

## What Is a QSE?

**QSE stands for Qualifying Small Enterprise** — a business with **annual total revenue between R10 million and R50 million**.

QSEs are measured against a simplified scorecard covering 4 of the 5 B-BBEE elements (they choose which 4). QSEs are generally not eligible to use sworn affidavits — they require formal verification.

However, there is one important exception under the generic Codes of Good Practice: a QSE that is **100% black-owned** may, with effect from 2023 Codes amendments, also use a sworn affidavit confirming 100% black ownership and automatic Level 1 status.

## When Can You Use a Sworn Affidavit?

### Case 1: EME — Turnover Under R10 Million

All EMEs may use a sworn affidavit in place of a formal B-BBEE certificate. The affidavit must:

1. **Be commissioned by a Commissioner of Oaths** (SAPS stations offer this for free; attorneys, notaries, and bank managers may also commission)
2. **State that the entity is an EME** (annual turnover does not exceed R10 million)
3. **State the black ownership percentage** (to claim Level 1 or Level 2)
4. **Be signed by a director, member, or sole proprietor** who has authority to bind the entity
5. **Not be older than 12 months** at the time of bid submission

A new affidavit is needed **every year**. Unlike formal certificates, affidavits do not have a fixed expiry date in the format of a certificate, but the convention in procurement practice is that an affidavit older than 12 months is considered stale.

### Case 2: 100% Black-Owned QSE (Post-2023 Codes)

Under the revised 2023 Amended Codes, a QSE with 100% black ownership may use a sworn affidavit to claim automatic **Level 1** status. This must be confirmed by the entity's financial records and the affidavit must:

- Explicitly state 100% black ownership
- Confirm QSE status (annual turnover R10M–R50M)
- Be commissioned by a Commissioner of Oaths
- Not be older than 12 months

**Caution:** Not all government accounting officers have updated their bid evaluation procedures to accommodate this 2023 change. Some evaluators continue to require formal certificates for QSEs even where the law permits affidavits. Always check whether the specific tender's special conditions or SBD forms specify which documentation is required.

## When Is a Formal B-BBEE Verification Certificate Required?

You need a formal B-BBEE certificate from a **SANAS-accredited rating agency** in the following cases:

1. **Your annual turnover exceeds R10 million** (you are a QSE or Generic entity)
2. **You are a Generic enterprise** (turnover over R50M — always requires full scorecard verification)
3. **The tender notice explicitly requires a formal certificate** regardless of your size
4. **The accounting officer has set a pre-qualification B-BBEE threshold** (e.g., Level 2 or higher required)
5. **You want to claim a specific measured scorecard level** rather than a default EME level

### Choosing a SANAS-Accredited Rating Agency

Only verification agencies accredited by the **South African National Accreditation System (SANAS)** can issue valid B-BBEE certificates. Certificates from non-accredited agencies are legally invalid and government buyers must reject them.

You can search for accredited agencies on the SANAS website (sanas.co.za). At the time of writing, there are approximately 90 accredited B-BBEE verification agencies in South Africa.

Costs vary by complexity:
- EME voluntary certificate: R1,500–R4,000
- QSE certificate: R8,000–R25,000
- Generic enterprise: R20,000–R100,000+

Turnaround time is typically 3–6 weeks for QSEs. Factor this into your tender planning.

## The Affidavit in Practice: Getting It Right

A defective affidavit is treated the same as no affidavit. Common errors that lead to disqualification:

**Error 1: Missing Commissioner of Oaths stamp**
The stamp, signature, and commissioner details must be clearly legible. Faded or incomplete stamps are rejected.

**Error 2: Stating the wrong entity name**
The legal entity name in the affidavit must match exactly what appears on the CSD and in your company registration documents (including the "(Pty) Ltd" or "CC" suffix).

**Error 3: Incorrect ownership claim**
If you claim Level 1 (51%+ black-owned), the ownership must be verifiable against your CIPC records and the beneficial ownership information in the CSD. Evaluators are increasingly cross-referencing these.

**Error 4: Affidavit signed by a non-director**
Only a director (for companies), member (for CCs), or the sole proprietor can sign the affidavit. An employee cannot sign on behalf of the entity.

**Error 5: Forgetting to submit SBD 6.1**
The affidavit alone is not sufficient. You must also correctly complete and submit **SBD 6.1** (the Preference Points Claim Form). The SBD form and the affidavit must be consistent.

## Affidavit Template Essentials

While there is no prescribed format, your affidavit should cover:

```
I, [Full Name], [ID Number], in my capacity as [Director/Member/Sole Proprietor]
of [Legal Entity Name], [Registration Number], hereby confirm under oath that:

1. The entity's annual total turnover does not exceed R10 million (EME status).

2. The entity is [51%+ / 30%+] black-owned as defined in the B-BBEE Codes
   of Good Practice, and accordingly qualifies as a B-BBEE Level [1/2]
   contributor.

3. The information contained herein is true and correct.

Signed at [City] on this [Date].

[Signature]
[Commissioner of Oaths stamp, signature, and reference number]
```

## Summary: Quick Reference

| Your Situation | Document Required |
|---|---|
| Turnover under R10M (EME), 51%+ black-owned | Sworn affidavit (Level 1) |
| Turnover under R10M (EME), 30%+ black women | Sworn affidavit (Level 2) |
| Turnover under R10M (EME), other | Sworn affidavit (Level 4) |
| Turnover R10M–R50M (QSE), 100% black-owned | Sworn affidavit (Level 1) — where accepted |
| Turnover R10M–R50M (QSE), other | SANAS-accredited certificate |
