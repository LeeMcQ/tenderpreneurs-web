South Africa's government procurement scoring system is governed by the **Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act (PPPFA), Act 5 of 2000**, and its associated Preferential Procurement Regulations of 2022. At the heart of this system is a deceptively simple question: out of a possible 100 points, how many go to price and how many go to empowerment criteria?

The answer depends entirely on the value of the contract.

## The Two-System Framework

The PPPFA establishes two preference point systems, and government buyers must apply the correct one based on the estimated value of the contract:

| Contract Value (incl. VAT) | Price Points | Preference Points |
|---|---|---|
| R30,000 to R50,000,000 | 80 | 20 |
| Above R50,000,000 | 90 | 10 |

This is the source of the shorthand "80/20" and "90/10" — referring to the price-to-preference split.

> **Important:** These thresholds apply to the **estimated contract value**, not your company's turnover. A small company can be evaluated under the 90/10 system if bidding for a large contract.

## Understanding the 80/20 System

The 80/20 system applies to contracts with an estimated value between **R30,000 and R50 million** (inclusive of VAT).

### Price Points (80)

The price component is calculated using a comparative formula:

```
Price Points = 80 × (1 – (Pt – Pmin) / Pmin)
```

Where:
- **Pt** = the price of the bid being evaluated
- **Pmin** = the lowest acceptable bid price

The lowest valid bidder always receives 80 points. Bidders with higher prices receive proportionally fewer points. There is no minimum — a very high bid could receive 0 price points.

**Example:**
- Bid A (lowest): R100,000 → receives 80 points
- Bid B: R120,000 → receives 80 × (1 – 20,000/100,000) = 80 × 0.8 = **64 points**
- Bid C: R150,000 → receives 80 × (1 – 50,000/100,000) = 80 × 0.5 = **40 points**

### Preference Points (20)

The remaining 20 points are allocated based on **B-BBEE contributor level**:

| B-BBEE Contributor Level | Points (out of 20) |
|---|---|
| Level 1 | 20 |
| Level 2 | 18 |
| Level 3 | 14 |
| Level 4 | 12 |
| Level 5 | 8 |
| Level 6 | 6 |
| Level 7 | 4 |
| Level 8 | 2 |
| Non-compliant | 0 |

B-BBEE status is verified against the bidder's certificate or sworn affidavit, as reflected on the CSD.

## Understanding the 90/10 System

The 90/10 system applies to contracts exceeding **R50 million** (including VAT).

### Price Points (90)

Same comparative formula applies, but with 90 as the multiplier:

```
Price Points = 90 × (1 – (Pt – Pmin) / Pmin)
```

### Preference Points (10)

| B-BBEE Contributor Level | Points (out of 10) |
|---|---|
| Level 1 | 10 |
| Level 2 | 9 |
| Level 3 | 7 |
| Level 4 | 6 |
| Level 5 | 4 |
| Level 6 | 3 |
| Level 7 | 2 |
| Level 8 | 1 |
| Non-compliant | 0 |

## The 2022 Preferential Procurement Regulations

The **Preferential Procurement Regulations, 2022** (gazetted on 16 January 2022 under Government Gazette No. 45826) introduced significant changes from the earlier 2017 regulations. The most notable changes relevant to the point system:

1. **Pre-qualification criteria**: Accounting officers may now require bidders to meet minimum B-BBEE requirements before their bids are considered. This is a powerful tool: a department can specify "Level 2 B-BBEE or higher" as a threshold, meaning non-compliant bidders are eliminated before scoring even begins.

2. **Subcontracting obligations**: For contracts above R30 million, organs of state may require the awarded supplier to subcontract a minimum of **30%** of the contract value to:
   - An EME or QSE with a minimum Level 4 B-BBEE status, AND
   - Which is at least 51% black-owned

3. **Exemptions**: The National Treasury retains the right to exempt specific procurements from the preference point system where it is impractical or not feasible.

## How This Affects Your Bidding Strategy

Understanding the point system changes how you approach competitive bidding:

### Scenario 1: Strong B-BBEE, Moderate Price

Under 80/20 on a R5 million contract:
- Your price is R5.5M (R0.5M above the R5M lowest bid)
- Price points: 80 × (1 – 500,000/5,000,000) = 80 × 0.9 = **72 points**
- Your B-BBEE: Level 1 = **20 points**
- **Total: 92 points**

Competitor with lowest price but Level 5 B-BBEE:
- Price points: **80 points**
- B-BBEE: **8 points**
- **Total: 88 points**

You win despite being 10% more expensive.

### Scenario 2: Large Contract, Thin B-BBEE Margins

Under 90/10, B-BBEE points are compressed — only 10 points separate a Level 1 from a Level 8. On large tenders, price competitiveness becomes overwhelmingly important. The difference between Level 1 and Level 4 B-BBEE is only **4 points** — easily erased by pricing just 4.4% lower than competitors.

### Scenario 3: Pre-qualification thresholds

If an accounting officer sets a minimum Level 2 B-BBEE requirement under the 2022 Regulations, your Level 4 certificate won't get you into the evaluation at all. Always read the tender notice carefully for any pre-qualification conditions in the Invitation to Bid (ITB).

## Common Mistakes Bidders Make

**Mistake 1: Confusing the threshold basis**
The threshold is the **estimated contract value**, not annual expenditure or your company turnover. A 3-year maintenance contract worth R60M total falls under 90/10.

**Mistake 2: Using an expired B-BBEE certificate**
Evaluators check the expiry date on your certificate. An expired certificate = non-compliant = 0 preference points. Worse, some accounting officers treat this as grounds for disqualification.

**Mistake 3: Failing to complete SBD 6.1**
The **SBD 6.1 form** (Preference Points Claim Form in terms of PPPFA) must be correctly completed and submitted with your bid. Missing or incorrectly completed SBD 6.1 forms result in 0 preference points being awarded — even if you have a valid Level 1 certificate.

**Mistake 4: Not understanding joint venture splitting**
In a joint venture, B-BBEE points are calculated based on the combined B-BBEE status of the JV, not individual members. The JV itself must have a B-BBEE certificate or combined affidavit.

## Special Cases

### Designated Groups

The 2022 Regulations maintain the concept of **designated groups** — women, youth (under 35), people with disabilities, and people living in rural areas or townships. Organs of state may set aside tenders exclusively for these groups, or apply additional preference points within the system for bids from designated groups.

### Secondary Industry (Local Content)

For certain goods, the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (DTIC) designates **locally produced content** requirements under the Preferential Procurement Regulations. For example:
- Clothing, textiles, and leather: 100% local content required
- Set-top boxes: 30% local content required
- Furniture: 85% local content required

Bids not meeting local content thresholds are automatically disqualified for designated products. This is separate from B-BBEE preference points.

## Practical Checklist for Preference Points

Before submitting your bid, verify:

- [ ] You know whether 80/20 or 90/10 applies (check contract value estimate in tender notice)
- [ ] Your B-BBEE certificate is valid (expiry date not passed)
- [ ] Your B-BBEE status on the CSD matches your certificate
- [ ] SBD 6.1 is completed correctly and signed
- [ ] If pre-qualification criteria exist, you meet them
- [ ] If subcontracting is required (30% on R30M+ contracts), you have identified your subcontractor and they are CSD-registered
