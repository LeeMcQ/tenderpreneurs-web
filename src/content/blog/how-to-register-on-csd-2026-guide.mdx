The Central Supplier Database (CSD) is the South African National Treasury's single source of truth for all government suppliers. If you want to bid for any government tender — whether at national, provincial, or municipal level — you **must** be registered and active on the CSD.

Without a valid CSD registration, your bid will be declared non-responsive before the evaluation committee even looks at your pricing.

This guide walks you through every step of the CSD registration process, common pitfalls to avoid, and how to keep your registration current.

## What Is the Central Supplier Database (CSD)?

The CSD was established under **Treasury Regulation 16A9.2** and launched in 2015 as a replacement for the fragmented supplier databases that existed across different government departments and entities.

Before the CSD, suppliers had to register separately with each organ of state — a time-consuming and duplicative process. The CSD centralises all supplier information, making it accessible to all government buyers through a single portal at **csd.gov.za**.

The database captures:

- Company registration details (verified against CIPC)
- Tax compliance status (verified against SARS)
- B-BBEE status (verified against SANAS-accredited rating agencies)
- Banking details (verified against FICA records)
- Contact information and physical address
- Directors and shareholders (beneficial ownership)

When a government official wants to invite suppliers to tender or verify a company's standing, the CSD is their first port of call.

## Who Must Register on the CSD?

Registration is required for:

- **All entities bidding for government tenders** — regardless of value
- **All entities contracting with organs of state** — including municipalities covered by the MFMA
- **Foreign companies** bidding for South African government work
- **Joint ventures** and **consortiums** — each member must be individually registered

Sole proprietors, partnerships, close corporations, private companies (Pty Ltd), and public companies are all eligible to register.

## Documents You Will Need Before You Start

Gather these documents before visiting the CSD portal. Having everything ready will save you significant frustration:

**Company Documents:**
- CIPC registration certificate (CM1 or CoR14.1 for Pty Ltd)
- Company resolution authorising the person completing registration (for companies with multiple directors)
- Proof of address not older than 3 months (municipal account, bank statement, or lease agreement)

**Tax Documents:**
- SARS Tax Clearance Certificate (or Tax Compliance Status PIN from eFiling)
- Company income tax reference number
- PAYE reference number (if you have employees)
- VAT registration number (if VAT-registered)

**Banking Documents:**
- Latest bank statement not older than 3 months (showing company name, account number, and branch code)
- Cancelled cheque or bank confirmation letter on bank letterhead

**B-BBEE Documents:**
- Valid B-BBEE certificate from a SANAS-accredited verification agency, OR
- Sworn affidavit (for EMEs with turnover under R10 million — more on this below)

**Beneficial Ownership:**
- Certified ID copies of all directors and shareholders with 25%+ interest
- Proof of identity for foreign directors (passport, not older than 3 months)

## Step-by-Step CSD Registration

### Step 1: Access the CSD Portal

Navigate to **csd.gov.za** and click "Register" on the top navigation. The portal uses a secure HTTPS connection — if your browser shows a certificate warning, do not proceed.

### Step 2: Create a User Account

Create a personal user account with your email address. This is separate from your company registration. One user account can manage multiple company registrations.

You will receive a verification email — check your spam folder if it does not arrive within 5 minutes.

### Step 3: Add Your Company

After logging in, click "Add New Supplier." You will need to select your entity type:

- Sole Proprietor
- Partnership
- Close Corporation (CC)
- Private Company (Pty) Ltd
- Public Company (Ltd)
- Non-Profit Organisation (NPO)
- Trust

Enter your CIPC registration number. The CSD will automatically query CIPC and pre-populate your company name, registration date, and director information. If this does not happen, verify your registration number on **cipconline.co.za** before proceeding.

### Step 4: Complete Business Information

Complete all mandatory fields marked with an asterisk:

- Trading name (if different from registered name)
- Physical address (where you operate from)
- Postal address
- Primary contact details
- Industry/sector classification (using the UNSPSC commodity codes — select all that apply to your business)
- Geographic area of operation

The UNSPSC (United Nations Standard Products and Services Code) classification is important. Buyers filter the CSD by commodity codes, so selecting the right codes determines which tenders you are visible for.

### Step 5: Upload Financial Information

Enter your annual turnover for the past 3 financial years. This is used to determine your B-BBEE category:

- **EME (Exempted Micro Enterprise):** Turnover under R10 million
- **QSE (Qualifying Small Enterprise):** Turnover R10–R50 million
- **Generic Enterprise:** Turnover above R50 million

### Step 6: B-BBEE Status Verification

For **EMEs** (turnover under R10 million), you can self-certify using a sworn affidavit. The affidavit must:
- Be commissioned by a Commissioner of Oaths (typically a police station or lawyer)
- State your annual turnover
- State your B-BBEE level (Level 1 for 51%+ black-owned, Level 2 for 30%+ black women-owned, otherwise Level 4 for all EMEs)
- Not be older than 12 months

For **QSEs and Generic enterprises**, you must upload a valid B-BBEE certificate from a SANAS-accredited rating agency. The certificate must show:
- Your B-BBEE contributor level
- Your procurement recognition level
- Expiry date

### Step 7: SARS Tax Compliance

The CSD links directly to SARS systems. Enter your SARS tax compliance status (TCS) PIN from your eFiling account. The CSD will verify your compliance in real-time.

If your tax affairs are not in order, this is where registration stalls. Before applying, log into eFiling and ensure:
- All returns are submitted
- No outstanding debt without a payment arrangement
- PAYE reconciliations are up to date

### Step 8: Banking Details

Enter your company banking details. These will be verified against FICA records. Ensure the account is in the company's name — personal accounts are not accepted for company registrations.

You will need:
- Bank name
- Account type (current/cheque recommended)
- Account number
- Branch code (or universal code for major banks)

### Step 9: Submit and Await Verification

Once submitted, your registration enters a verification queue. The CSD verification process typically takes **5–10 business days**, though this can extend to 3–4 weeks during peak periods.

You will receive email notifications at each stage. Your CSD number will be issued once verification is complete.

## Understanding Your CSD Status

Once registered, your supplier profile will show one of these statuses:

- **Active:** Registration complete and verified — you can bid
- **Inactive:** Registration exists but not verified — you cannot bid
- **Expired:** One or more elements (tax compliance, B-BBEE) have expired
- **Deregistered:** Company has been removed (CIPC deregistration or court order)

Buyers check your status at the time of bid submission. An expired status at that point will disqualify your bid.

## Keeping Your CSD Registration Current

Your CSD registration is not a once-off exercise. You must update it whenever:

- Your SARS tax compliance status changes
- Your B-BBEE certificate expires (typically annual)
- Company directors change
- Banking details change
- Physical address changes
- You change CIPC registration status (e.g., name change, share restructuring)

Set a calendar reminder 60 days before your B-BBEE certificate expires. Late renewal is the single most common reason for bid disqualification.

## Common CSD Registration Errors and How to Fix Them

**Error: "CIPC number not found"**
Verify your registration number at cipconline.co.za. Common issues include mistyping the registration year prefix or confusing a CC number with a company number format.

**Error: "Tax compliance status could not be verified"**
Log into your SARS eFiling account and generate a fresh TCS PIN. Old PINs expire after 21 days.

**Error: "Banking details could not be verified"**
Contact your bank for a formal confirmation letter on bank letterhead, signed by an authorised representative. Some banks charge for this service.

**Error: "B-BBEE certificate expired"**
If your existing certificate has expired, you cannot proceed without a new one. For EMEs, a fresh sworn affidavit is the fastest solution.

## CSD and Tender Bid Submission

When completing bid forms (particularly SBD 1 and SBD 4), you will need to provide your **CSD supplier number**. This is typically formatted as MAAA followed by 8 digits.

Some tender notices require you to print your CSD supplier profile report as an attachment. You can access this from within your CSD account under "Supplier Report."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I register on the CSD without a CIPC company registration?**
Yes. Sole proprietors do not need CIPC registration. You will register using your RSA ID number and trading details.

**Q: How long does CSD registration take?**
Typically 5–10 business days after submission of complete documentation. Incomplete applications are returned for correction, resetting the clock.

**Q: Is CSD registration free?**
Yes. Registration on csd.gov.za is completely free. Be wary of third parties charging for CSD registration assistance.

**Q: Can I bid for tenders while my CSD registration is being verified?**
No. You must have an Active status before submitting any government bid.

**Q: How many CSD registrations can I have?**
One registration per legal entity. One user account can manage multiple entity registrations.
