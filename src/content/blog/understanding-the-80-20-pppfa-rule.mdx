The Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act (PPPFA) and its 2022 regulations govern how organs of state evaluate tenders using a points system that balances price and socio-economic objectives. The most widely applied formula is the 80/20 preference point system, used for tenders with a value below R50 million (including VAT).

## How the 100 points break down

Under the 80/20 system, a total of 100 points is allocated. Up to **80 points are reserved for price**, and a maximum of **20 points may be awarded for specific goals** linked to the tender.

Price points are calculated using a relative scoring formula: the lowest acceptable bid gets the full 80 points, while other bids receive points proportionally. For example, if Bid A quotes R1 million and Bid B quotes R1.2 million:

- Bid A scores 80
- Bid B scores (1 000 000 / 1 200 000) × 80 = 66.67 points

Accuracy in pricing and completeness of the financial offer is therefore paramount.

## The 20 points are not automatic

The remaining 20 points are not automatic. They are allocated only if the bidder attains stated specific goals, which the organ of state must clearly articulate in the tender documents. The 2022 regulations include mandatory goals: contracting with companies that are at least:

- 51% black-owned
- 30% black-woman-owned
- 30% black-youth-owned
- 7% black-people-with-disabilities-owned

Organs of state may subdivide the 20 points among these categories and, where justifiable, add local content or other socio-economic objectives. If a bidder does not meet any goal, they score zero out of 20, regardless of price.

## When the 80/20 rule applies

A critical nuance is that the 80/20 rule applies only to tenders above R2 000 and below R50 million. For contracts of R50 million or above, the 90/10 system kicks in.

The 2022 regulations also tighten the link between B-BBEE and preference points. An organ of state may not award preference points solely on the basis of a B-BBEE level certificate unless the tender specifically applies the B-BBEE dimension as one of the measurable specific goals. The contract must stipulate how B-BBEE status contributes to the 20 points, and the bidder must submit a valid certificate or sworn affidavit to claim those points.

## The functionality threshold

Calculating the final score is straightforward: price points out of 80 plus specific goal points out of 20. The tender must be awarded to the bidder with the highest total points, unless objective criteria justify otherwise.

However, the regulations also demand that the winning bid passes the functionality threshold. If a tender includes functionality criteria, only bids scoring above a minimum quality hurdle proceed to the preference point stage. This prevents a pure race to the bottom on price.

## Practical advice

Procurement practitioners must document the evaluation of specific goals meticulously. The score sheet must reflect how each bidder's claimed goals were verified, whether through the CSD B-BBEE indicator, affidavits, or other evidence. Any mathematical error in price scoring can be a source of dispute, so independent checking is advisable.

For bidders, the lesson is to study the tender's specific goal matrix and assemble all supporting proofs before submission. A missing signed affidavit could mean forfeiting up to 20 points — the difference between winning and losing.
