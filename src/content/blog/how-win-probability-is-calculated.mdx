Winning a government tender is often seen as a lottery, but smart bidders calculate their win probability before committing resources. Win probability estimation combines a realistic assessment of the competition, analysis of the evaluation criteria, and honest introspection about the company's strengths and gaps. It is both an art and a science, and it is critical for go/no-go decisions.

## Step 1: Dissect the criteria

The first step is to dissect the evaluation criteria and their weightings. In a typical 80/20 tender with functionality, the total points are split: a minimum functionality threshold must be passed, then 80 points for price and 20 for specific goals.

Functionality is often scored out of 100, with a threshold of, say, 70 points. If your own honest self-assessment against the published scoring matrix gives you only 65 points, your win probability is zero, irrespective of price. That saves you the cost of preparing a full bid.

## Step 2: Estimate your price score

If you clear functionality, estimate your likely price score. Gather intelligence on your competitors' likely pricing. If you have bid against the same firms before, their historical margins and price models are a reference point.

Assume the lowest bidder will set the benchmark and earn 80 points. If your estimate is 15% higher than that forecast lowest price, your price score will be roughly (Lowest / Yours) × 80. For instance, if the expected lowest bid is R2 million and your price is R2.5 million, your price score is (2/2.5) × 80 = **64 points**.

## Step 3: Calculate your specific goal points

Examine the matrix closely. If the tender allocates:

- 10 points to B-BBEE Level 1, and you hold Level 2 → 9 points
- 5 points to locality, and you are within the municipal area → 5 points
- 5 points to women ownership, and you qualify → 5 points

Total: **19 out of 20**. A competitor with a Level 1 and similar locality might get the full 20.

## Step 4: Combine and contextualise

Combine the numbers: functionality (pass), price score (64), specific goals (19) gives a total of 83 points out of 100. Now you must place this in a competitive field.

You do not know the exact number of bidders, but you can estimate how many capable firms will respond. If you think there are three competitors who can beat your price and match your specific goals, your win probability would be lower than 25%. But if only one competitor has a clear price advantage, you might still win if your specific goals edge them out.

## The compliance attrition factor

Sophisticated bidders use weighted decision matrices and even Monte Carlo simulation where the spread of possible lowest prices is modelled. However, at a practical level, the biggest factor is often disqualification.

If your bid is fully compliant and others commonly make errors, your actual win probability rises. Research shows that in many government tenders, 20–30% of bids are non-responsive. Factoring in this "compliance attrition" can boost your calculated odds.

## Use it for discipline

Win probability is not a guarantee, but it drives discipline. If the figure is below 15%, consider whether the tender is a strategic reference or a profit-making opportunity. Use it to focus on tenders where your company's profile aligns tightly with the evaluation criteria, and where the price gap is bridgeable.

Calculated bidding turns procurement from a gamble into a portfolio of measured risks.
