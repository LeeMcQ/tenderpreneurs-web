You spent weeks preparing your bid. Your pricing was competitive, your technical proposal was strong, and you were confident in your B-BBEE credentials. Then you received the letter: *"Your bid has been found non-responsive and has been disqualified at the administrative compliance stage."*

This is one of the most frustrating experiences in government procurement. The good news is that administrative disqualification is almost entirely preventable once you understand the rules.

Here are the five most common reasons South African tenders get thrown out before evaluation — and exactly how to fix them.

## Reason 1: Invalid or Expired Tax Clearance

Tax compliance is non-negotiable in South African government procurement. **Treasury Regulation 16A9.1(d)** requires that all bidders for government contracts have a valid Tax Clearance Certificate at the time of bid submission.

### What evaluators check

Evaluators verify your Tax Compliance Status (TCS) directly with SARS systems. An expired certificate on paper is bad enough — but even a valid-looking certificate may fail if your underlying compliance has lapsed.

Your TCS can become invalid if:
- You missed a return submission deadline
- An outstanding tax debt has not been settled or subject to a payment arrangement
- PAYE reconciliations are outstanding
- You have an active audit that has resulted in a tax liability assessment

### The fix

Before submitting any bid:
1. Log into **SARS eFiling** and navigate to Tax Compliance Status
2. Request a fresh TCS PIN — this is live data from SARS, not a document that can become stale
3. Verify your TCS PIN generates a "Compliant" result
4. Enter this PIN into your bid documents (SBD forms often have a dedicated field)
5. Print and attach the TCS confirmation from eFiling

**Critical tip:** TCS PINs expire after 21 days. Generate yours within 3 weeks of the tender closing date, not months in advance.

## Reason 2: CSD Registration Issues

Since 2015, all suppliers to organs of state must be registered on the **Central Supplier Database (CSD)** at csd.gov.za. An inactive or expired CSD registration results in immediate disqualification.

### The four CSD statuses and what they mean for bidders

- **Active:** You can bid. ✅
- **Inactive:** Registration exists but not verified. ❌ Cannot bid.
- **Expired:** One or more components (typically tax compliance or B-BBEE certificate) has lapsed. ❌ Cannot bid.
- **Deregistered:** The entity has been removed from the CSD. ❌ Cannot bid.

### Common reasons CSD status lapses

**B-BBEE certificate expiry** is the single most common culprit. Most B-BBEE certificates are valid for 12 months. When your certificate expires, your CSD profile automatically drops to an "Expired" status — even if everything else is current.

**SARS TCS linkage:** The CSD links directly to SARS. If your tax compliance lapses, your CSD status updates accordingly within days.

**CIPC changes:** If you've changed company directors, converted from CC to Pty Ltd, or changed your registered address at CIPC without updating the CSD, your records become inconsistent and your CSD status may flag for verification.

### The fix

1. Check your CSD status at **csd.gov.za** at least 60 days before any bid submission
2. Set recurring calendar reminders for B-BBEE certificate renewal — 60, 30, and 14 days before expiry
3. After any CIPC changes, log into CSD and update your profile within 30 days
4. After renewing tax compliance, allow 48–72 hours for CSD to update automatically

## Reason 3: Incomplete or Incorrectly Completed SBD Forms

Standard Bid Documents (SBDs) are the required forms that accompany every government bid. National Treasury publishes a standard set, and accounting officers may add department-specific forms. Every mandatory form must be completed correctly and submitted.

### The forms that most commonly trip up bidders

**SBD 1 — Invitation to Bid**
Often overlooked because it looks like a cover page. It is not. It must be signed by an authorised signatory. Unsigned SBD 1 = disqualification.

**SBD 4 — Declaration of Past Supply Chain Management Practices**
This requires the bidder to declare any conflicts of interest, unsolicited offers, or improper conduct. It must be signed under oath. "Under oath" means it needs a Commissioner of Oaths stamp — not just a signature.

**SBD 6.1 — Preference Points Claim**
This is where bidders claim their B-BBEE points under the PPPFA. Errors here don't just affect your preference score — some accounting officers treat a fundamentally defective SBD 6.1 as grounds for disqualification. Common errors: claiming the wrong B-BBEE level, not attaching the supporting certificate or affidavit, or leaving the form blank.

**SBD 8 — Contractor Performance Declaration**
Required for construction-related bids. Must disclose any previous contract terminations, penalties, or litigation with government.

**SBD 9 — Certificate of Independent Bid Determination**
Anti-collusion declaration. Every bidder and all directors must sign. If a single director's signature is missing and the form requires all directors, this can be flagged.

### The fix

Create a **bid submission checklist** specific to each tender. Before the closing date:

1. Print the complete list of required SBDs from the tender notice
2. Check that each form is completed in full — no blank mandatory fields
3. Ensure signatures are from authorised persons (company resolution may be required to evidence authority)
4. Confirm which forms require a Commissioner of Oaths attestation
5. Do a final check the night before submission

## Reason 4: Failure to Meet Technical Pre-Qualification Requirements

Many tenders specify **technical pre-qualification criteria** that must be met for a bid to be considered. These are pass/fail gates — you either meet them or you don't.

### Common technical pre-qualification requirements

**CIDB Registration (for construction tenders)**
The Construction Industry Development Board (CIDB) grades contractors from Grade 1 (up to R200K contracts) to Grade 9 (unlimited). Bidding for a Grade 7 contract without a Grade 7 CIDB registration results in automatic disqualification. The required grade is always stated in the tender notice.

**Professional or trade association membership**
Some tenders require bidders to be registered members of specific bodies: ECSA (engineering), SACAP (architecture), SAIA (insurance advisors), etc. If the tender requires membership of a specified body and you are not a member, you cannot bid.

**Minimum experience thresholds**
Tenders frequently specify minimum years of experience or a minimum number of similar projects completed. You must provide evidence (client letters, certificates of completion, or purchase orders) for each claimed project.

**B-BBEE pre-qualification level**
Under the 2022 PPPFA Regulations, accounting officers may set a minimum B-BBEE level as a pre-qualification criterion. If a tender requires Level 2 or higher B-BBEE and you are a Level 4, you will be disqualified at pre-qualification — before price and preference points are even calculated.

### The fix

Before spending time on any bid, read the **Special Conditions of Contract** and **Invitation to Bid** carefully. Build a simple table:

| Pre-qualification criterion | Required | Our status | Evidence available |
|---|---|---|---|
| CIDB Grade | Grade 6 | Grade 6 ✅ | CIDB registration cert |
| B-BBEE Level | Level 3+ | Level 2 ✅ | Certificate attached |
| Similar projects | 3 in past 5 years | 5 projects ✅ | CPO letters |

If any row shows ❌, do not submit. Bidding when you know you don't meet pre-qualification criteria is a waste of resources.

## Reason 5: Late Submission or Incorrect Submission Format

Government tender closing times are absolute. **There is no grace period.**

### The hard rules on timing

A bid submitted one minute after the closing time — even due to traffic, load shedding, or a server error — will be returned unopened. This is not a discretionary rule; it is a compliance obligation under SCM procedures derived from **PFMA Section 38(1)(a)(iii)** on internal controls.

**Physical submissions:** The bid must be in the marked tender box, sealed envelope with the tender number clearly marked, before the closing time. Bids left at reception, given to a security guard, or placed on a desk are not valid submissions.

**Electronic submissions (where offered):** ePortals such as eTenders have their own technical requirements. Uploads must be complete before the server-stamped closing time. File size limits, format requirements (usually PDF), and password protection can all cause technical failures.

### Format requirements that trip bidders up

- Required to submit **3 original copies**: submitting 2 originals and 1 photocopy can be flagged
- **Page numbering required**: some RFPs specify continuous page numbering with specific separators between sections
- **File format for electronic submissions**: PDF is almost universally required; DOCX or Excel files may be rejected
- **Original signatures required**: photocopied signatures on declarations may be rejected

### The fix

1. Submit at least **24 hours early** whenever possible — this allows you to address any physical or technical problems
2. For physical submissions, visit the venue in advance to confirm the exact location of the tender box and parking/access arrangements
3. For electronic submissions, conduct a **test upload** of a dummy file well before the deadline to confirm the portal works with your browser and internet connection
4. During load shedding periods, do not rely on your internet connection — upload from a fixed-line connection or during a scheduled "on" period

## Your Pre-Submission Checklist

Use this checklist for every government tender:

**Documents & Compliance**
- [ ] Tax Compliance Status verified on eFiling (TCS PIN generated within 21 days)
- [ ] CSD registration Active — checked at csd.gov.za
- [ ] B-BBEE certificate valid (expiry date checked)
- [ ] CIDB registration current (for construction bids)
- [ ] Professional body membership current (if required)

**SBD Forms**
- [ ] SBD 1 signed by authorised person
- [ ] SBD 4 commissioned by Commissioner of Oaths
- [ ] SBD 6.1 completed and supporting B-BBEE documentation attached
- [ ] All other required SBDs completed, signed, and attached

**Submission**
- [ ] Sealed in correct envelope with tender number clearly marked
- [ ] Correct number of copies prepared
- [ ] Submitted to correct location before closing time
